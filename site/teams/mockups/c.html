1

The list changes. The training follows it.

A photo or a PDF of the wine list and the menu. Vinterest reads it and shows what's new, what's gone and what changed: a new vintage, a new price, by the glass now. Nothing reaches staff until the manager says Publish.

A new arrival is photographed from its label. Its card, its pairings and its drinking window are on the floor in about 45 seconds, assigned to everyone who sells it.

A new vintage is the same wine moving on, so nobody relearns it. Every publish is a version you can look back at.
2

One card per wine, written for the floor.

What to say to a guest, in plain words. What it tastes like, with body, acidity, tannin and sweetness each explained in a line, because not everyone on a Friday rota went to wine school. Grape and place, how to serve it, which dishes on this menu, and what to offer instead from this list.

Every dish gets its best wines, the by-the-glass pick, and the one to steer guests away from. All of it grounded in checked facts about the region and the grape, and the manager can rewrite the line in the house's own words.

3

Five questions a day, built for remembering.

Each wine has four rungs: recognise it, recall it, apply it at a table, and tell it from the most similar wine on the list. A rung opens once the one below is right. Servers need three; the sommelier and the buyer all four.

Every question comes back on its own schedule. They say how sure they are before answering: a confident wrong answer returns within hours, a right guess grows slowly. About one question in ten is an unannounced re-check, so what the team remembers is measured honestly.

No points, no streaks. The motivation is not being caught out.
Readiness comes only from drills. Sales change what people learn first, never what they're said to know.
The one rule the console is built on
4

The manager sees who's ready before service.

On a tablet or a laptop the same app is a console. Readiness per person against the minimum for their role: a server needs 80% of the glass list, a sommelier 90% of everything. Who's overdue, who's slipping, which new wines nobody knows yet, which by-the-glass wines too few people can sell. Every number opens the people or wines behind it.

5

The till says what to teach first.

Import a night's sales from any till as a CSV today; the Square connector comes next. What sells most and is known least goes to the top of everyone's drill. The console shows what sells, who sells it, the slow movers by the glass with the card's line to say beside each, and whether a trained server sells more of a wine than one who isn't, with the honest caveat that shifts and sections confound it.
