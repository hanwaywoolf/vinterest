What each problem means and how to fix it:
- giveaway: the question holds its own answer, offers it as one of two choices ("drunk young or aged?"), or one word picks the answer (the wine's name says "Rosé" and only one option does). Ask about something the name and question don't reveal, with no either/or. A "recognise" question's four options must all be the same kind of wine as this one (four rosés for a rosé) and must not repeat a word of the question that the others lack.
- cue: the right option is much longer or shorter than the others, so it picks itself out. Rewrite the options so all four are about the same length and in the same form.
- nochoice: a table question names its own wine or sets a request every wine of that kind on the list meets, so it tests nothing but the reason. Rewrite it as the apply shape below: the guest's request rules out three named decoys.
- thin: too few believable wrong answers exist for it. Ask instead about something with plenty of real alternatives on this list or in wine generally: a grape, a place, a flavour, a price by the glass or the bottle, a style. For "contrast", ask how the two wines differ in one concrete way (grape, place, body, price), with an answer short enough to swap between them.
- mismatch: a guest asks for this wine with a dish it doesn't suit. Never call a light white or pale rosé right for a rich braise or red meat.
